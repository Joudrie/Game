# The vision (owner, after v34)

What this game is, in one line: **Hitman, but loud.** You're with the **Vanguard**, you drop into a mission on a planet, and you can do it quietly and perfectly or blow the whole base up. Either way is fun. Play solo or with a friend.

This replaces "no plot, just a sandbox" (v21). The sandbox stays as a place to test things, but the game is the missions.

## The best five minutes (the owner's picture)
A friend is the Jedi and you're the guy with the gun. You sneak in and the stealth goes well. Then the Jedi pushes when he meant to pull, knocks a guard into an exploding barrel, the whole base goes on high alert, and it all goes wrong in a fun way.

**Every mission should be able to produce that moment.** Stealth that works, things in the level that can go off, and a loud fallback that's just as fun as the quiet plan.

## Ground rules
- **Tone:** Modern Warfare 2 serious in look and guns: real-looking, real-feeling weapons. But the consequences are over the top, with a bit of a **flash-game vibe**: a grenade makes a guy explode. Dismemberment stays, but it isn't graphic or cruel. Enemies are faceless nobodies.
- **Dying is no big deal.** Respawn fast. Blowing yourself up should be funny, not punishing. Checkpoints are optional, for players going for a perfect run.
- **Score, over the top:** points pop up for barrel kills, silent kills, Force kills, combo chains. **A trophy for a perfect stealth run** (never spotted).
- **Story is loose.** Short and silly, never in the way.
- **Your Lego figures are the inspiration, not the look.** The characters are based on the sci-fi and Jedi figures you made as a kid, built as realistic models. Nothing should look like Lego.
- **The *Wake* universe, not canon:** its planets, tech and weapons, used loosely.
- Everything from the v21 rules still holds: the saber is lethal, enemies die fast (difficulty comes from numbers), no enemy health bars, no weight system, PC first, duo co-op PvE someday.

## The Vanguard and the story
- You're part of **the Vanguard** (not "the Guard", the National Guard's name).
- **The plot is a chain:** a rogue military is making alliances with an evil faction on each planet. You go and take them out, one planet at a time.
  - Example: Mission 1, cargo was stolen. Take out the alien raiders who took it. It turns out they're working with someone bigger who has better guns. On to the next planet.
- **No main villain**, at least for now. Each planet has its own bad group, and the military is behind them.
- **Silly cutscenes, in the engine, short:** you walk into a room, a trap springs, and the bad guy on a screen laughs "ha ha, you're trapped." You can keep moving during them.

## Missions
- **Types:** stealth, assassinate, assault, sabotage, steal the cargo, rescue, survive. The three missions built so far (Infiltration, Survive, the assault) are early versions of these.
- **One map per mission, each with its own planet look:** rocky, lava, desert, snow, jungle, city…
- **Size depends on the mission.** Some maps are big and fast (jetpack, grapple). Some are small and tight, where you can't rush. Small and full of stuff beats big and empty.
- **Several paths through each base:** a loud way, a quiet way, and a class way (a Jedi can Force-jump onto a roof; a soldier can blow a breach in the wall). Easy, chill paths are fine; they just have to be fun.

## Classes
You load into a mission as a class, and it changes how you play the level:
- **Infantry:** guns, grenades, gadgets, cover, breaching.
- **Jedi:** saber, Force, big jumps. Stealth works differently: you deflect, you choke quietly, you take the high route.
- Maybe later: a stealth specialist (holocloak, suppressed guns, disguises).

## Things in the level that go wrong (shenanigans)
Every base should have some of these lying around:
- exploding barrels (a push, a shot or a grenade sets them off; chain reactions)
- turrets: get past them, destroy them, or take one over
- landmines
- lakes and rivers of lava (push guards in; don't fall in yourself)
- alarms you can cut before anyone uses them
- vehicles to drive or blow up
- destructible walls (already built: stone, wood and glass)

## Enemies talk
- **Chatter while calm:** guards talk to each other as you walk past (GTA-style overheard lines).
- **Barks in a fight:** "Reloading!", "Grenade!", "Man down!", screaming when on fire or missing an arm. Lots of variants so it doesn't repeat.
- **Where the voices come from:** you and friends record them (best, and they're yours), plus free licensed voice packs or free text-to-speech to fill gaps. Short lines, compressed, to fit the size budget.

## How we build the bases
Building is the hard part. The plan:
1. **A modular kit,** not one model per base: walls, floors, stairs, gates, towers, crates, barrels, turrets, lava tiles, from free packs (Kenney, Quaternius, KayKit) so they snap together.
2. **An in-game base editor,** grown from the creative catalogue: place pieces, enemies and patrol routes, then save the layout to a file. You build and tweak bases inside the game itself.
3. **Claude drafts, you adjust:** describe a base, or sketch it on paper or in Minecraft as a top-down plan, and Claude makes a first layout; you fix it in the editor.

Minecraft is good for sketching the plan, but its blocky look doesn't fit real-looking guns, so its models don't go in the game.

## First milestone: one mission, done properly
Before more maps, make one mission that delivers the five minutes above:
**Mission 1, "The cargo yard"** (a rocky planet): the raiders' depot with the stolen cargo.
- a quiet path, a loud path and a Jedi path
- exploding barrels, a turret, an alarm panel, a landmine field
- guard chatter and combat barks
- score popups, and the perfect-stealth trophy
- one silly trap cutscene
- the ending reveals who the raiders work for

If that's fun with a friend, the rest is more maps and more factions.
